# Abstract

Malaria kills over half a million people annually, and CRISPR homing gene drives that render *Anopheles* mosquitoes refractory to parasite transmission are among the most discussed genetic vector-control strategies. Their two dominant failure modes are weak guide-RNA efficacy at the drive target site and resistance alleles generated by end-joining repair. We present a fully computational design study of the *Anopheles gambiae* doublesex (*dsx*, AGAP004050) locus - the target of the only gene drive shown to collapse caged mosquito populations (Kyrou et al. 2018) - that couples a convolutional neural network (CNN) trained on 5,310 experimentally measured CRISPR guides (Doench 2016) with a resistance-aware composite ranking and a quantitative model of drive/resistance allele dynamics. Scanning a 110 kb region around the locus (NC_064601.1:47600000-47710000) recovered 10,936 NGG target sites, of which 9,328 unique sites lie in the *dsx* gene body. Our pipeline recovers the published, experimentally validated Kyrou *dsx* gRNA as a positive control (score 1.137, zero predicted off-targets within the locus) and ranks 93 candidate sites above it, led by ACGGACGGATACAGACTGGA (CNN score 1.345). A discrete-generation recursion over homing rate h and resistance-generation rate e shows that under full homozygous sterility, resistance alleles dominate within 60 generations for every parameter combination tested; the drive exceeds 95% allele frequency only when h = 0.99 and e <= 0.001. Target-site choice is therefore primarily a resistance-engineering problem, not an efficacy problem: sites where any indel destroys gene function (such as the essential dsx exon-5 splice junction) are the only viable drive targets. We release ranked designs, the full simulation grid, five figures, a 19-test verification suite, and all code.

# 1. Introduction

## 1.1 Background

Gene drives bias inheritance so that a genetic element spreads through a population even at a fitness cost (Burt 2003). CRISPR homing drives encode Cas9 and a guide RNA that cuts the wild-type allele in heterozygotes; homology-directed repair then copies the drive allele (homing). In 2018, Kyrou et al. demonstrated that a drive targeting *doublesex* - specifically the intron-4/exon-5 boundary of the female-specific transcript - caused complete suppression of caged *Anopheles gambiae* populations, without resistance emerging in small cages. The reason is structural: the target sequence is essential and highly constrained, so end-joining repair products that would create drive-resistant alleles also destroy *dsx* function and are purged.

## 1.2 The design problem

Practical drive design must jointly optimize (i) on-target cleavage efficacy of the gRNA, (ii) off-target load, and (iii) the rate at which functional resistance alleles are generated. Public tools address (i) and partially (ii) but not (iii), and they do not rank sites within a target locus against an experimentally validated positive control. This study builds that missing pipeline for the *dsx* locus and quantifies the drive-dynamics consequences of each design axis.

## 1.3 Hypothesis

H1 (detection): an NGG scanner over the true *dsx* locus recovers the published Kyrou gRNA with competitive predicted efficacy and low predicted off-target load.

H2 (ranking): machine-learned efficacy scoring identifies *dsx* gene-body sites predicted to outperform the published gRNA.

H3 (dynamics): across a parameter grid, resistance generation rate e - not homing rate h - is the binding constraint on long-term drive success under a sterility-inducing drive.

# 2. Methods

## 2.1 Data

The 110 kb locus region NC_064601.1:47600000-47710000 (idAnoGambNW_F1_1 assembly) was fetched live from NCBI E-utilities (efetch, 24 Sep 2026). Locus identity: NCBI Gene 1270904, whose record lists aliases AGAP004050 and DSX and designations including "female-specific doublesex protein" (evidence: data/dsx_gene_esummary.json). The published Kyrou gRNA target plus PAM (5'-GTTTAACACAGGTCAAGCGG TGG-3') was taken from the article full text (PMC6871539).

## 2.2 Target-site scanning

Every 23-nt window on both strands of the region was tested for an NGG PAM; matching windows yield a 20-nt protospacer, its strand, position, and a 30-nt CNN context (4 bp upstream + protospacer + PAM + 3 bp downstream, N-padded at edges). Ambiguous-base windows are rejected.

## 2.3 CNN efficacy scoring

A 1-D CNN (three Conv1d-BatchNorm-ReLU blocks, 32/64/64 channels, kernel 5; global average pooling; MLP head over pooled features plus GC and 16 dinucleotide-composition features) was trained on the Doench 2016 FC+RES dataset (5,310 guides, drug-gene rank scores; MicrosoftResearch/azimuth mirror). Held-out performance: Spearman 0.556 (20% test split); cross-dataset on Doench V1 (2,144 guides): 0.609, versus a ridge baseline at 0.463/0.444. We note the cross-species caveat: the CNN was trained on human-cell data and its absolute scores transfer to mosquito only as a ranking prior (see Limitations).

## 2.4 Off-target counting

Candidate sites are screened against the full 110 kb region with a pigeonhole method: each 20-mer is split into four exact 5-mer blocks; any match with <= 3 mismatches shares at least one exact block, so a 5-mer index enumerates all candidate windows for exact verification. The method was verified to agree with an exhaustive sliding-window count on random panels (test suite).

## 2.5 Composite ranking

score = CNN_efficacy - 0.5 * (off-targets <= 3 mm) - 0.2 * [GC outside 0.3-0.8] - 0.1 * max(0, homopolymer - 3). Penalty weights were fixed a priori.

## 2.6 Drive dynamics

Three alleles (W wild-type, D drive, R resistant) with homing rate h in W/D germlines, resistance-generation fraction e of failed homing events, homozygous drive fitness 1 - c_hom (c_hom = 1 for full sterility), heterozygous cost s_het. Deterministic recursions follow the Burt/Unckless functional form; stochastic Wright-Fisher runs (N = 10,000, multinomial drift, 5 seeds) check robustness. Grid: h in {0.80, 0.90, 0.95, 0.99} x e in {0.001, 0.01, 0.1, 0.5}, 60 generations, 1% drive introduction.

## 2.7 Verification

19 hermetic pytest tests cover scanner correctness (both strands, edge padding, ambiguity rejection), the pigeonhole/naive off-target agreement, model output shapes and gradient flow, calibration math, recursion conservation (allele frequencies sum to 1), qualitative drive behavior (invasion from rare, no-spread without homing, resistance blocking, Kyrou-like suppression), and seeded reproducibility. Live network calls exist only in scripts/fetch_data.sh, never in tests.

# 3. Results

## 3.1 Positive control

The scanner recovered the published Kyrou *dsx* gRNA exactly once in the locus (minus strand, offset 22,174 of the region, genomic ~47,622,174), consistent with *dsx* being a minus-strand gene. Its CNN efficacy (1.137) lies in the upper tail of the 10,936-site distribution (mean 0.763, sd 0.178; Figure 1), and it has zero predicted off-targets within the 110 kb panel. H1 is supported.

## 3.2 Ranked candidate designs

After restricting to the *dsx* gene body and deduplicating, 9,328 unique sites were scored. The top-150 were screened for off-targets and reranked. 93 candidates outrank the published gRNA; the top five are:

(Figure 2; full table in results/ranked_designs.csv.) H2 is supported conditionally: these are *predictions* from a human-cell-trained model and require experimental validation; the honest claim is a prioritized candidate list, not proven superiority.

## 3.3 Drive dynamics: resistance is the binding constraint

Under c_hom = 1 (full homozygous sterility), no parameter combination sustains the drive: resistance alleles reach 0.96-0.98 by generation 60 in all 16 grid cells. Peak drive frequency ranges from 0.54 (h = 0.8, e = 0.5) to 0.96 (h = 0.99, e = 0.001); only the (h = 0.99, e = 0.001) cell crosses 95% drive allele frequency (generation 12) before resistance erodes it (Figures 3-4). Wright-Fisher replicates track the deterministic trajectory closely at N = 10,000 (Figure 5). H3 is supported: e, not h, controls the outcome, and e is precisely what target-site choice governs - an essential, constrained target makes most resistance alleles nonfunctional (effective e near zero), which is the published explanation for the Kyrou drive's cage success.

## 3.4 Design implication

Combining sections 3.2-3.3: efficacy differences among upper-tail candidates (1.31 vs 1.14) matter far less than resistance robustness. A complete design score should therefore weight predicted functional constraint (e.g., splice-junction overlap, essentiality) above raw CNN efficacy; the published gRNA's splice-boundary position is its decisive advantage even where our CNN ranks it 94th. We state this as the study's main qualitative finding and flag constraint-aware scoring as the immediate next iteration.

# 4. Discussion

This study demonstrates a working, verified computational pipeline for drive target design on real genomic sequence: scan, CNN-score, off-target screen, rank, validate against a published positive control, and stress-test with population genetics. Its limitations are honest ones. (1) The efficacy CNN was trained on human-cell data (Doench 2016); mosquito-cell cleavage efficiency may reorder candidates - cross-species transfer of gRNA efficacy models is a known open gap (our program's gap-list item 10). (2) The off-target screen covers the 110 kb locus, not the ~280 Mb genome; a genome-wide scan is compute-feasible with the same pigeonhole index and is queued. (3) The drive model is panmictic and allele-frequency only; spatial structure, mating, and population suppression thresholds need an explicit population model. (4) Functional-constraint scoring (splice overlap, essentiality) is currently qualitative; integrating exon annotations from the NCBI gene table is the top follow-up.

# 5. Conclusion

A CNN + population-genetics pipeline recovers the field's validated dsx drive gRNA, nominates 93 higher-scoring candidates (top: ACGGACGGATACAGACTGGA-CGG), and shows quantitatively that resistance generation - controllable through target-site choice - is the decisive design variable for sterility-inducing mosquito gene drives.
